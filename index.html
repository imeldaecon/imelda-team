Current Projects

1
Surviving the Interruption 🇿🇦 South Africa

This project examines how electricity outages reshape daily life and economic behavior of firms in emerging economies. Using supply chain freight data and mobile location records from South Africa, it traces how infrastructure shocks propagate through logistics networks and shift urban mobility patterns. The findings reveal how unreliable electricity supply imposes costs far beyond immediate disruption, reshaping firm decisions, household routines, and service-sector activity across space.

2
Crime in the Dark 🇿🇦 South Africa

This project investigates how scheduled electricity outages reshape the geography and incidence of criminal activity. Exploiting the predictable timing of load shedding, it shows that outages raise the return to criminal activity through darkened streets and disrupted policing. A central question is whether outages increase aggregate crime or spatially displace it.

3
Coal Mining & Electricity Shortage 🇿🇦 South Africa

This project traces South Africa's electricity reliability crisis back through the supply chain, where 80% of generation depends on coal-fired plants tied to rigid, mine-specific fuel contracts. It documents how upstream rainfall shocks — via flooded mines and transport disruptions — reduce plant availability through unplanned outages, directly contributing to load shedding. These findings carry a broader lesson: in coal-dependent systems with inflexible input markets, climate shocks are already binding reliability today, with costs unaccounted for in standard climate damage functions.

4
Mobility Responses to Planned Outages 🇿🇦 South Africa

This project examines how sudden electricity outages reshape daily life using mobile location records from South Africa, it traces how infrastructure shocks propagate through logistics networks and shift urban mobility patterns.

5
Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) 🇮🇩 Indonesia

This project studies how smart metering technology affects electricity consumption, compliance in emerging economies with historically weak payment enforcement. Using administrative data from Indonesia's state-owned utility — one of the largest AMI rollouts in Southeast Asia — it estimates how households respond to tariff changes as they transition to smart meters, and whether AMI could act as a signaling of enforcement.
